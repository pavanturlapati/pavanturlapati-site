No account. No sign-up. Just a text box.

That is all it takes for anyone, including a school-going child, to start using one of the most powerful technologies ever built. And even with an account, a child can simply pick up a parent's logged-in phone, and the AI will assume it is talking to an adult.

As a parent, that stopped me. A child is still learning why effort and understanding matter, yet can now get a polished answer to almost anything in seconds.

So I keep asking: did the child learn, or did the child just get the result?

And with teachers and parents stretched thin, who really has the time to check?

This isn't about any one company. It's about every AI provider. I don't think the answer is banning AI. I think it is shared responsibility, and AI companies have to go first:

- Every under-18 account linked to a parent or guardian, by default
- Guest mode and adult accounts that notice a likely minor and ask a parent to approve
- AI that mentors instead of just answering, with the parent's agreement
- Parents kept in the loop on key moments, like being asked before a party or a sleepover

Parents, teachers, schools, governments and society each have a role too. I've set out my suggestions in the article.

The test I'd apply: after using AI, is the child more capable of the next task alone, or only of submitting this one?

Should every under-18 AI account be linked to a parent? I'd like to hear what you think.
