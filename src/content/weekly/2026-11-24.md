## The big three

1. **Anthropic launched Claude Ship**, a set of agents that review pull requests, fix problems and, in beta, approve routine changes under policies a team writes.
2. **The UK's NCSC reports a "marked rise" in AI-driven attacks** that exploit old, unmitigated weaknesses rather than new vulnerabilities.

## AI

### Anthropic launches Claude Ship for review, fixes and approvals

Anthropic announced Claude Ship, a suite of agents for reviewing, fixing and approving routine code changes. It combines Code Review, which is already generally available, with two beta agents. PR Steward deals with failing CI checks, merge conflicts and Code Review feedback, and comments on what it changed and how to roll it back. Claude Approvals applies an approval policy that the team writes in natural language as a file in the repository. Anthropic suggests starting in observe-only mode, with one repository and monthly spending limits, and says teams decide which changes are escalated to a human. It is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans for repositories on GitHub.

**Why it matters:** if approval authority is delegated to an agent, the written policy and the escalation rules become something that needs the same scrutiny and testing as code.

*Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/) · 3 min read · Nov 23*

### UK warns AI-driven attacks are exposing years of weak security

The UK National Cybersecurity Center says it has seen a "marked rise" in AI-driven attacks, with several incidents since September involving autonomous hacking tools. These caused intrusions and downtime at organizations. The NCSC says the vulnerabilities being exploited are not new, and describes them as unmitigated weaknesses that are often the result of years of technical debt. According to the NCSC, the tools look for exposed internet-facing systems, try weak or easy-to-guess passwords and abuse API endpoints. The NCSC says AI does not make established controls obsolete, but that weaknesses unnoticed for years can now be found in minutes.

**Why it matters:** this suggests basics such as password hygiene, exposed endpoints and API access controls deserve checking, because the cost of finding gaps has dropped.

*Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/uk-warns-ai-driven-cyberattacks-are-exposing-years-of-weak-security/) · 3 min read · Nov 24*

### Tool Retrieval arrives in the Gemini API

Google announced Tool Retrieval in preview in the Gemini Interactions API. Instead of sending every tool's full parameter schema with each request, an agent keeps its library on standby and loads a schema only when needed. Developers add a tool_search tool and set defer_loading to true on the tools to hold back. Google says this reduces input tokens and latency and avoids lower tool-selection accuracy as the tool list grows. It works with Gemini 3.6 and later models, has no separate charge, and Google advises measuring savings against your own traffic.

**Why it matters:** agents with large tool sets are a cost and accuracy problem, and the steps are visible in the interaction, which helps when debugging tool choice.

*Source: [Google AI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-api-tool-retrieval/) · 5 min read · Nov 19*

### GPT 6.2 Astra and an anonymous 1808 sketchbook

Simon Willison links to a case study by history professor Mark Humphries. Humphries says GPT-6.2 Astra, with minimal prompting, identified the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook at Yale's Beinecke Library in 22 minutes, using public sources. He says it found a little-known archive source and predicted that examining it would confirm its hypothesis, which it did. Humphries expects historians to face a milder crisis of confidence than mathematicians. This is one historian's account, relayed in a short link post.

**Why it matters:** it is a single anecdote, but it shows agents being used for open-ended research where the result can be checked against a source.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/24/astra-historical-mystery/) · 2 min read · Nov 24*

### How seven restaurant chains are using AI

Restaurant Dive rounds up recent AI moves by chains. It says Hi Auto's drive-thru technology is at 267 Checkers & Rally's restaurants, heading toward half the system by early next year. McDonald's says its automated ordering system could save 50 weekly labor hours, though some franchisees are unsure customers want it. Bojangles says its voice ordering handles 1 million orders a week across 500 restaurants with 98% accuracy. Those are company figures. The piece also notes Starbucks' AI stockkeeper lasted nine months.

**Why it matters:** the numbers are chain claims, so the gap between announced and observed results is the thing to watch.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/7-restaurant-chains-deploy-artificial-intelligence/875198/) · 3 min read · Nov 20*

### Starbucks says inventory availability is at an all-time high

Starbucks CEO Brian Niccol said on the fiscal Q4 earnings call that the company has made progress on daily deliveries and expects to meet replenishment goals in six to 12 months. He said availability is at an "all-time high" and daily deliveries reach nearly 90% of company-operated North American coffeehouses. The article notes Starbucks dropped an AI and computer vision inventory pilot after nine months because employees found it unreliable, and moved to a single inventory process. Testing continues on demand forecasting and ordering accuracy.

**Why it matters:** a simpler, consistent process is what is credited here, not the AI pilot, and the results are the CEO's own statements.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/starbucks-inventory-availability-at-all-time-high-with-daily-deliveries/875178/) · 2 min read · Nov 23*

### Is the industry leaving small franchisees behind?

The third part of a Restaurant Dive series says power is shifting toward major brands and large franchise operators. A Bank of America executive says about 19% of franchisees operate 60% of all units. Economists quoted say franchisor control has risen, and that technology now gives franchisors close to real-time insight into every restaurant. Examples include Burger King's "Patty" voice assistant, which coaches workers, and a franchisee lawsuit over Pizza Hut's Dragontail system. Restaurant Dive's analysis of 30 large chains found the three fastest-growing accounted for 41% of net unit growth in 2025.

**Why it matters:** technology rolled out by franchisors changes what franchisees can control, which matters for anyone designing or testing systems across franchise operators.

## Testing

### Martin Fowler's fragments: code health, agent pace and verification

Fowler highlights an experiment by Marcus Borg on whether healthy code is cheaper for agents to review. It covered three LLMs and 465 pull requests across 246 open source repositories, with health measured by CodeScene. The post says the difference between typical unhealthy and healthy pull requests adds up to 22 to 35%, and names Complex Method and Complex Conditional as costly smells. Fowler also quotes Steve Yegge urging teams to pace agents to human speed, and Chad Fowler's argument that code generation is no longer the bottleneck, verification is.

**Why it matters:** this is a quoted experiment, but it points to code quality and verification tooling as practical levers for agent-heavy teams.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-11-20.html) · 7 min read · Nov 20*

### Data Contracts

Simon Harrer and Arif Wider describe data contracts as a way to specify the contents and guarantees of datasets, applying API design practices to data. Their example uses the Open Data Contract Standard, with schema, quality rules written as checks and service levels such as freshness. They argue this gives people and AI agents explicit metadata to work with, and suggest keeping contracts in Git and in CI/CD pipelines. One author is CEO of a company selling a data marketplace built on data contracts. This summary covers only the opening part of the article.

**Why it matters:** quality rules and freshness guarantees written down per dataset give testers something concrete to check against.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/data-contracts.html) · 33 min read · Nov 18*

## Takeaway

Agents are taking on more of the work after code is written, and attackers are using autonomous tools against basic weaknesses. In both cases the controls that matter are plain ones: clear policies, healthy code, and checks that can be verified.
