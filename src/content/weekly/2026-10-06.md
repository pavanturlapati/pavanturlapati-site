## AI

### Mistral releases its 1T-parameter Large 4

Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4, a multimodal model with 1 trillion parameters, on Tuesday. It is not open-weight yet. For now it is available only through a public guardrail endpoint, and Mistral says it plans to release the weights in three weeks, after safety testing. Mistral VP Science Pierre Stock says the model was trained on 4,000 Nvidia GPUs, which he describes as two to three times fewer than Chinese competitors use. Benchmark results are still pending, so the hope that it will be best in class among open-weight models is Mistral's own claim.

**Why it matters:** until benchmarks and the weights appear, this is a statement of intent, so any evaluation will have to wait.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/mistrals-new-1t-model-aims-to-leapfrog-closed-and-open-rivals/) · 3 min read · Oct 6*

### Simon Willison: default hard budget caps

Willison argues that pay-by-usage services should offer hard monthly caps that cut service off and return errors, and that these should be on by default, with an opt-in to remove them. His reasoning is that coding and personal agents make it easy to spin up code that spends money. He notes that AWS announced spending limits on 16 September, though AWS's own page says the new experience is going to a limited number of customers. He also says Google Cloud launched a similar feature, Spend Caps, in July.

**Why it matters:** if agents can create costs unattended, spend limits become a basic control to check before letting them deploy anything.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/) · 3 min read · Oct 3*

### Matthew Green on agents and worms

Simon Willison quotes security researcher Matthew Green, who describes the two halves of a worm: a payload that hijacks an agent, and an agent that carries it to the next one. Green says agents in separately isolated sandboxes found they could leave instructions for each other in a shared package cache, and those instructions changed what the recipients did. He argues that replacing the cache with email, Slack, shared documents or WhatsApp, and the training runs with independently deployed personal agents like Muse, gives a worm exactly what it needs.

**Why it matters:** this suggests that isolating each agent may not be enough if agents can read the same shared channels.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/1/matthew-green/) · 1 min read · Oct 1*

### Uber Eats to buy ezCater for $2.3B

Uber Eats agreed to acquire the catering platform ezCater in an all-cash deal worth $2.3 billion, according to a joint press release. The deal is expected to close within the next few months. According to the company, ezCater works with over 140,000 restaurants, had over $2.5 billion in gross bookings in the past 12 months and has grown in the high teens year over year. The press release did not say how ezCater will be integrated or whether it will remain a separate business.

**Why it matters:** large catering orders are becoming a competitive channel among delivery platforms.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/uber-eats-acquires-ezcater-2-billion/832219/) · 2 min read · Oct 6*

### DoorDash launches DashOS

DoorDash unveiled DashOS, which ties together its marketplace, direct ordering, reservations, loyalty, guest management and marketing tools, according to a press release from its Dash Forward event. DoorDash says it works without restaurants overhauling their tech, and lists integrations including Clover from Fiserv, Qu, Checkmate, Chowly, Deliverect, Stream and Urban Piper. DoorDash claims its AI recommendations could increase reservations by up to 15%. That figure is the company's own claim.

**Why it matters:** combining customer data across channels raises the importance of how well these integrations work.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/door-dash-dashos-ai-tool-rewards/831793/) · 2 min read · Oct 1*

### Ways restaurants can reduce the impact of fuel costs

At an event hosted by Restaurant Dive and Supply Chain Dive, consultants suggested restaurants look at delivery frequency and drop size, route optimization and old freight contracts. Bill Krogstad of FTI Consulting said moving a store from five deliveries a week to four brings fuel savings. Dheera Anand of Bain & Company pointed to early warning systems and scenario planning. The article cites Energy Information Administration data showing gasoline at $4.47 and diesel at $6.38 per gallon as of Sept. 29.

**Why it matters:** these are practical levers, and visibility into late shipments and costs is part of them.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/how-restaurants-can-mitigate-rising-fuel-costs/832163/) · 3 min read · Oct 5*

## Testing

### Playwright 1.63 adds test locks

This is a catch-up item from Sept 4. Playwright 1.63 lets a test declare a named lock, and tests sharing a lock name never run at the same time across files, workers and projects. Other tests keep running in parallel. The release also adds a visible-only locator, frame locators that search any frame without locating the iframe first, and aria and screen snapshots in traces. It notes that Ubuntu 20.04 is no longer supported.

**Why it matters:** locks look like a more targeted fix for flaky tests that share state than reducing everything to one worker.

*Source: [Playwright releases](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/releases/tag/v1.63.0) · 4 min read · Sep 4*

### Principles for effective slides

Writing on Martin Fowler's site, Sumeet Gayathri Moghe says slides should complement the speaker rather than replace them. He argues that slides should be tightly coupled to what the speaker says without repeating it, and that you should never send slides out in advance. He also says not every presentation needs slides.

**Why it matters:** this is relevant to anyone presenting quality or delivery reports, where a slide that reads like a document can pull attention from the speaker.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/never-send-slides/slide-principles.html) · 13 min read · Sep 30*

## Takeaway

Several stories this week concern what automated systems can do on their own, from agents passing instructions to each other to AI-recommended prices and runaway cloud bills. Clear limits, such as spend caps and test locks, help keep that behavior contained.
