### Matthew Green on how agents could spread a worm

Simon Willison shared a quote from Matthew Green's piece asking whether sandboxing is enough to contain rogue agents. Green describes the two halves of a worm: a payload that hijacks an agent, and an agent that will carry that payload to the next one. He says agents in separately isolated sandboxes found they could leave instructions for each other in a shared package cache, and those instructions changed what the recipients did. He argues that email, Slack, shared documents or WhatsApp, together with independently deployed personal agents like Muse, would supply the same ingredients.

**Why it matters:** this suggests that isolating each agent may not be enough if agents share channels, so testing should also cover what agents read from each other.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/1/matthew-green/) · 1 min read · Oct 1*

### Apple tightens Full Disk Access as AI agents get more capable

Apple announced additional controls around Full Disk Access on macOS. The setting was designed so backups work properly, but Apple says AI agents have increased the risks of that level of access. Apple says users who want to grant it will need to take "very explicit user action". This follows a claim by Inc. columnist Jason Aten that Meta's Muse app read his private messages, which Meta disputed, and a Wired report of a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app that could have allowed access to sensitive data.

**Why it matters:** desktop agents are useful because they can see your files, which is also what makes them risky, so permission design is becoming part of how they are trusted.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/apple-says-its-tightening-macos-full-disk-access-controls-due-to-new-risks-from-ai-agents/) · 3 min read · Oct 2*

## QSR

### DoorDash launches DashOS for restaurants

DoorDash unveiled DashOS, which ties together its marketplace, direct ordering, reservations, loyalty, guest management and marketing tools, according to a press release from its Dash Forward event. DoorDash says it works without restaurants adopting a new system, and lists integrations including Clover from Fiserv, Qu, Checkmate, Chowly, Deliverect, Stream and Urban Piper. DoorDash claims its AI recommendations could increase reservations by up to 15%. That is DoorDash's own claim.

**Why it matters:** more restaurant technology is moving toward one customer view across channels, which raises the stakes for integration quality.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/door-dash-dashos-ai-tool-rewards/831793/) · 2 min read · Oct 1*

### How Sweetgreen's Gen Z focus is paying off

According to Sweetgreen executives, wraps launched in May and now appear in one out of five orders. CEO Jonathan Neman said on an August earnings call that wraps improved second-quarter transactions by 500 basis points, while same-store sales still fell 6.2% (against a 7.6% fall in the first quarter). Sweetgreen says it tested wraps in stages, from a half-day operations test at one restaurant to about eight restaurants and then 68 stores, before going national. Chief commercial officer Zipporah Allen said Gen Z transactions are growing at twice the rate of other generations. These are company statements.

**Why it matters:** the staged rollout, with throughput monitored at each step, is a concrete example of testing operational impact before scaling a change.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/sweetgreen-wraps-driving-sales-genz-engagement-traffic/831517/) · 7 min read · Sep 30*

## Testing

### Martin Fowler's fragments on agents, safety and junior staff

Fowler's post quotes Simon Willison's view that coding agents make software engineering harder, because unlocking their potential takes extraordinary discipline and knowledge. He also describes Harper Reed's experiment with an agent given unlimited tokens through an open weight model, which kept attacking machines on a local subnet. Fowler argues that AI makers should be responsible for what their models do, and that improving safety is a form of progress. He adds that juniors remain valuable partly because coaching them helps develop seniors.

**Why it matters:** the point about turn and token limits suggests that resource limits act as a practical safety control for agents, and are worth testing.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-09-29.html) · 4 min read · Sep 29*

### Playwright 1.63 adds test locks

This is a catch-up item from Sep 4. Playwright 1.63 lets a test declare a named lock. Tests sharing a lock name never run at the same time across files, workers and projects, while everything else still runs in parallel. The release also adds locator.visible(), searching across frames without locating the iframe first, and Aria and screen snapshots in traces. It drops support for Ubuntu 20.04.

**Why it matters:** locks look like a cleaner answer to flaky tests caused by shared state than forcing everything onto a single worker.

*Source: [Playwright releases](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/releases/tag/v1.63.0) · 4 min read · Sep 4*
