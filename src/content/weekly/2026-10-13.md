## The big three

1. **Google launched Gemini 4 Argon**, its latest frontier model, available through the API and in Google's consumer and subscriber products from today.
2. **American Express introduced the Intent Intelligence Framework**, which captures and authenticates a customer's purchase intent before an AI agent completes a transaction.
3. **A Martin Fowler article reports an experiment on behavioral harnesses**: a cheaper model succeeded in 3 of 10 runs without one and 10 of 10 with one.

## AI

### Google releases Gemini 4 Argon

Google says Gemini 4 Argon is globally available today for developers and enterprises via the API, for Google AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, and for Ultra and Pro subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Google says it ranks #1 on the Vals Index and that the introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens at 95% off. Google also reports that Argon in Antigravity helped teams increase agentic code submissions by more than 35% while reducing code rollbacks by nearly a third. It also reports a 40% cut in in-memory session prep time in its Agent Development Kit. All of these figures are Google's own claims. The text supplied was cut off partway through.

**Why it matters:** the benchmark and productivity numbers come from the vendor, so treat them as a starting point for your own evaluation rather than a conclusion.

*Source: [Google AI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/) · 13 min read · Oct 13*

### Amex proposes a framework to stop unauthorized agent purchases

American Express introduced the Intent Intelligence Framework, which it hopes will become an industry standard. A Create Intent API captures the customer's instructions and constraints, such as merchant, maximum budget and product preferences. The customer then authenticates and signs off, and an Intent ID and a Proof of Intent Token are created. The token is used to obtain a secure digital payment credential that the agent uses at checkout instead of the real card number. Amex says it has not yet seen an increase in disputes from agentic commerce, and that it is in discussions with partners across the payments ecosystem.

**Why it matters:** a logged, verifiable record of what a user authorized is the kind of control that makes agent behavior checkable, which is useful to anyone designing tests for agentic flows.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/amex-introduces-a-framework-to-prevent-ai-agents-from-making-unauthorized-purchases/) · 5 min read · Oct 13*

### Meta releases Muse Wonder, its first frontier model

Meta's Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full. According to Meta's own testing in its model card, they achieve benchmark scores comparable to Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens through the Meta Model API. Wonder Full is only available to Muse Code subscribers and is not served through the API. Meta emphasizes a property it calls "active memory" and says Wonder scores higher on MemoryBench than Fable, Opus or Astra.

**Why it matters:** the comparisons are Meta's own, so the practical question is how the model performs on your tasks at this price.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/meta-releases-its-first-frontier-model-muse-wonder/) · 3 min read · Oct 12*

### Claude Haiku 5.5 priced to match GPT-6 Luna

Simon Willison writes that Anthropic's new Haiku 5.5 is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens up to 100,000 tokens, matching GPT-6 Luna. Beyond 100,000 tokens the price rises to $0.50 and $2.50. He also found that its new tokenizer uses around 1.25 times as many tokens as Haiku 4.5 for the same long prompt. Separately, Anthropic is halving the price of cache reads for Sonnet 5.5 and adding monthly API credits for Max and Team subscribers. This is a catch-up item from Oct 7.

**Why it matters:** per-token prices alone can mislead, since tokenizer changes and price tiers by prompt length affect the real cost.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/7/claude-haiku-5-5/) · 4 min read · Oct 7*

### Friendliness and speed drive drive-thru satisfaction

A QSR and InTouch study sent mystery shoppers on 2,145 trips across 13 brands. When service was friendly, shoppers reported overall satisfaction 99% of the time, falling to 83% when service felt neutral and about 27% when it felt not friendly. Dutch Bros Coffee, Chick-fil-A and Starbucks ranked as the friendliest brands and also highest for overall satisfaction. Satisfaction was 97% when the order arrived quicker than expected and 82% when it was slower than expected. This is a catch-up item from Oct 9.

**Why it matters:** perceived speed and warmth both move satisfaction, so service-quality measures that track only speed may miss part of the picture.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/drive-thru-feels-fast-friendly-satisfaction-grows/832477/) · 2 min read · Oct 9*

## Testing

### Are harnesses worth the cost?

Naresh Jain compared coding agents building the same API service with and without a behavioral harness of guides and sensors. GPT-6 Sol passed all 178 acceptance tests in all 10 runs either way, but the harness used about 10.5 times as many tokens and cost about 7 times more. GPT-6 Luna succeeded in 3 of 10 unharnessed runs and 10 of 10 with the harness, at 33% less cost per run than Sol ($0.273 vs $0.406). The author notes the brief was unusually detailed and that results with a less complete brief are unknown. The supplied text was cut off partway through.

**Why it matters:** this is a single experiment on one task, but it suggests that executable checks may help cheaper models more than frontier ones.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/are-harnesses-worth-the-cost.html) · 18 min read · Oct 13*

### Playwright 1.64 adds WebMCP, shuffle and Locator.within()

Playwright 1.64 adds page.webmcp and frame.webmcp for testing tools a page registers through the experimental WebMCP browser API. The test runner gains a --shuffle option to find tests that depend on each other, and a default option to keep a project in the config without running it by default. A new locator.within() combines two locators, for example a button within a dialog. Breaking changes include the screen property now being forwarded from device descriptors and elements inside hidden iframes being considered hidden. This is a catch-up item from Oct 7.

**Why it matters:** the breaking changes are worth checking before upgrading, and --shuffle is a low-effort way to surface hidden test coupling.

*Source: [Playwright releases](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/releases/tag/v1.64.0) · 6 min read · Oct 7*

## Takeaway

Vendor model claims continue to arrive with their own benchmarks, which are worth verifying against your own work.
