## AI

### OpenCode breach runs up charges on users' AI accounts

OpenCode, an open source AI coding agent, said attackers broke into an older system overnight, affecting 1.4% of users of its online platform, OpenCode Console. Some users store API keys there. Developers reported unauthorized charges, with some in the hundreds of dollars and a few above $1,000. Anthropic and Google blocked the keys they believed were exposed, and OpenAI said it worked with OpenCode to restore access and refund its API customers. OpenCode called it a distillation attack, but TechCrunch notes that some skeptics say that framing is unproven. OpenCode also said its open source app and command-line tool were not affected, and that the incident is ongoing. The article says OpenCode's AI tools sometimes refused to analyze the malicious code because of safety filters, until it got access to OpenAI's Daybreak Blue program.

**Why it matters:** API keys stored in a shared platform are a spending and security risk, so it is worth knowing where your team's keys live and how quickly they can be revoked.

### Microsoft says AI agents should be treated like insider threats

Microsoft introduced new security features for enterprises, led by Guardian Agents, which are meant to monitor the AI agents a company uses. Microsoft's security business chief Hayete Gallot wrote that frontier models "cannot be trusted", not because they are malicious, but because they are unpredictable. CEO Satya Nadella has written that enterprises need to separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it. Microsoft also added a Defender feature that it says can find up to 60 types of agents employees bring into the workplace, and controls that limit agent access and require human approval for risky decisions. TechCrunch calls the approach both surprising and self-serving, since Microsoft wants enterprises to buy agent security from it.

**Why it matters:** the idea that agent monitoring should be independent of the agent supplier is likely to shape how teams think about controls and evidence for agent behavior.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/microsoft-says-ai-agents-are-like-insider-threats-as-frontier-models-cant-be-trusted/) · 4 min read · Oct 19*

### Review as spellcheck: how the Claude Code team does code review

Simon Willison quotes Addy Osmani, Morgan Westlee Lunt and Qing Wang from their piece on how the Claude Code team does code review. They say "did a human read the code?" is an imprecise proxy for "what could go wrong with this change, and which check is the right one to catch it?" In their description, review is no longer a gate at the end but runs continuously, and most changes have been reviewed and fixed by Claude at least once before a human is asked to look.

**Why it matters:** this frames review as a question of which check fits which risk, which is a useful lens for quality leaders deciding where human attention goes.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/) · 1 min read · Oct 16*

### Anthropic builds its own source control

Simon Willison highlights an Anthropic piece on why it built a custom alternative to Git. Anthropic says it averaged around 2,500 merged code changes per day in May 2026 and over 30,000 per day as of September. The new source control system is called Scion, and the code review system is called Antique. Anthropic says a team of seven built it and the migration from Git took five months. Willison guesses that newer model classes are a key factor in the faster pace, but that is his speculation. Anthropic also notes that adversarial review can be myopic, so skilled engineers had to steer Claude away from over-engineering.

**Why it matters:** if change volume rises this sharply, tooling and review practices built for human-paced work may become the bottleneck.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/13/redesigning-source-control-at-anthropic/) · 3 min read · Oct 13*

### Google launches Gemini 4 Argon

Google says Gemini 4 Argon is available now to developers and enterprises through its API and to paid Gemini subscribers. It says the model ranks #1 on the Vals Index and launches at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens 95% off. Google also says that in Antigravity, Argon helped teams increase agentic code submissions by more than 35% while reducing code rollbacks by nearly a third. These are Google's own claims, not independent results.

**Why it matters:** the pricing and the internal engineering figures are vendor claims, so they are worth checking against your own workloads before relying on them.

*Source: [Google AI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/) · 13 min read · Oct 13*

## QSR

### Whataburger diversifies suppliers to curb inflation

Whataburger's Chief Supply Chain Officer Todd Huetinck told Restaurant Dive the chain expanded its beef and chicken suppliers, vertically integrated with beef packers where possible, and began sourcing some non-food products globally. Whataburger forecasts 2026 inflation will come in under 2%, which Huetinck said is significantly lower than the national average food basket. He says the company held safety stock in the U.S., so tariff impact was "very minimal". He also warned that focusing only on short-term risks can become a "dangerous death cycle".

**Why it matters:** the forecast is the company's own, but the approach of targeting the highest-inflation categories and keeping buffers is a concrete example of supply chain resilience.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/whataburger-supply-chain-diversification-efforts/833287/) · 5 min read · Oct 20*

### How restaurant managers use AI, according to Toast data

This is sponsored content from Toast, based on its own 2026 Restaurant AI Report covering Toast IQ usage in Q2 2026. Toast says fine dining managers sent 67% more prompts per location than fast casual managers, sales and revenue questions were 30% of threads, 54% of interactions started on mobile, and 53% of prompts came during peak lunch or dinner service. The report's methodology notes say it measures prompts, not whether any resulting actions were completed, and that topic categories are approximate.

**Why it matters:** it suggests usage clusters around live service, but it is a vendor-sponsored analysis of one product's prompts, not evidence of outcomes.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/spons/how-restaurant-managers-use-ai-findings-from-toast-iq-data/833056/) · 4 min read · Oct 19*

### Why small restaurants struggle with inventory counts

In an opinion piece, Jim Lange, founder of a restaurant procurement software company, argues that food cost depends on an easily wrong inventory count. He lists common errors: unit confusion, inconsistent handling of partial containers, count sheets that do not match storage order, timing mismatches with deliveries, and counting too rarely. His suggested routine includes one unit per item, a rule for partials, a clean invoice cutoff, two people counting, and weekly counts of high-value items.

**Why it matters:** the same principle applies to any metric, since a calculation is only as trustworthy as the data collection feeding it.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/jim-lange-proper-inventory-counting-routines-reduce-erros/833140/) · 4 min read · Oct 19*

## Testing

### When software meets hardware: new sensible defaults

Kuldeep Singh describes modernizing a retail IoT system with around 800,000 battery-powered sensors across 3000 stores. His team built an IoT lab and a "sensor farm" of real devices so that physical behavior became part of development and testing. He says practices like TDD and automation still applied, but with hardware in the loop. He also says devices needed built-in diagnostics so they could explain themselves when out of reach. The text supplied for this item appears to end partway through the article, so this summary covers only the opening sections.

**Why it matters:** for hardware-dependent systems, test environments may need real devices and built-in diagnostics, not just software tooling.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/software-meets-hardware.html) · 14 min read · Oct 15*

### Playwright 1.64.1 fixes two regressions

Playwright 1.64.1 is a patch release with two bug fixes. One addresses _electron.launch intermittently losing a packaged app's first window since 1.64.0. The other addresses Chromium 155+ aborting a preflighted cross-origin redirect whenever any route is registered.

**Why it matters:** teams on 1.64.0 that test Electron apps or use route mocking may want to review whether these issues affect them.

*Source: [Playwright releases](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/releases/tag/v1.64.1) · 1 min read · Oct 14*
