## AI

### GitHub Octoverse 2026 shows a sharp rise in activity

Simon Willison, attending GitHub Universe, relays numbers that GitHub executive Jay Parikh highlighted from this year's Octoverse report. Merged pull requests tripled in less than a year to 220 million per month. Commits more than tripled to 7.38 billion per month, mostly in the second half of the year. The rate of new repositories doubled to 30.3 million per month. Willison calls this a stark representation of the impact of agentic engineering on GitHub. These are GitHub's own figures.

**Why it matters:** if this volume of change is real, review, testing and delivery checks have far more to absorb, which is where quality teams are likely to feel it.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/28/github-octoverse-2026/) · 2 min read · Oct 28*

### Anthropic is still grappling with the economics of compute

According to TechCrunch, an updated Anthropic S-1 filing extends its results through September 30. Revenue reached $17 billion, up from $11.5 billion the previous quarter, but compute took 58% of revenue, up from 48%. The filing says $5.0 billion of the $10 billion compute bill was under short-term commitments that cost significantly more than multi-year ones. TechCrunch says many indications suggest the margin shift is more of a blip than a long-term problem, and it also reports that Anthropic expects compute to remain its largest operating expense for the foreseeable future.

**Why it matters:** the price and availability of AI tooling depend on providers' compute costs, so it is reasonable to plan for pricing that can change.

*Source: [TechCrunch AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-still-grappling-with-the-economics-of-compute/) · 4 min read · Nov 3*

### How researchers took over ChatGPT accounts

Simon Willison summarizes a write-up by Parameter Labs of an exploit against ChatGPT's HTML previews feature, which shows custom HTML apps in a sandboxed iframe. The attack chained a postMessage handler that used innerHTML in a virtual keyboard library, an injected unsandboxed iframe, and a YouTube oEmbed endpoint allowed by the content security policy. The result was that a shared ChatGPT URL could exfiltrate the private data of a signed-in visitor or act on their behalf. OpenAI closed the hole and paid a $5,000 bounty, which Willison thinks was cheap for something this serious.

**Why it matters:** the attack worked by combining several individually modest weaknesses, which is a useful reminder for security testing of features that render untrusted content.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/31/how-we-took-over-chatgpt-accounts/) · 2 min read · Oct 31*

### Building a multiplayer game with autonomous agents

Simon Willison highlights Jacob Sansbury's account of Spawn, a multiplayer game where players change the world by prompting an in-game agent. Sansbury says a swarm of agents develops it, costing over $3 million in tokens per month, and ships to production hourly. He says an hour without a ship is treated as a SEV-0 incident and that broken changes can be rolled back in 30 seconds. Willison says he has trouble imagining this working for software with higher stakes. These figures come from the company's own description.

**Why it matters:** it shows what a fully automated feedback and rollback loop looks like at the extreme, and that it depends on strong monitoring and recovery paths.

*Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/how-i-built-the-most-ai-pilled-company-in-the-world/) · 3 min read · Oct 29*

## QSR

### FTC and states sue Toast over fees

The Federal Trade Commission and eight states, including California, sued Toast on October 29, alleging hidden fees, subscriptions that are hard to cancel, and unmet support promises. The lawsuit also says Toast obscured a 99-cent fee on online orders, which ended in July 2023. Toast says it disagrees, will defend itself vigorously, and does not believe the ROSCA law applies to negotiated business-to-business agreements. These are allegations, not findings.

**Why it matters:** restaurant operators may want to re-read subscription, add-on and cancellation terms with their technology vendors.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/ftc-states-sue-toast-over-fees-restaurant-dining-online-food/873286/) · 4 min read · Oct 30*

### Starbucks plans more personalization for 2027

On its Q4 earnings call, CEO Brian Niccol said Starbucks plans to make its app more intuitive, add personalized menu recommendations, and use AI for search and discovery. He said delivery will be integrated into the app in the second quarter of next year, with points earned and redeemed on delivery orders. Starbucks also plans to test AI to help baristas with orders and to explore recognizing customers at the drive-thru. Global comparable sales rose 7.2%, according to the earnings release.

**Why it matters:** app, loyalty, delivery and drive-thru changes all touch each other, which raises the testing burden across channels.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/starbucks-greater-personalization-on-menu-2027/873364/) · 3 min read · Nov 3*

### Chipotle Rewards drives a record share of sales

Chipotle CEO Scott Boatwright said on the Q3 call that active membership passed 24 million and members accounted for one-third of sales, the highest share ever. The program was overhauled in April. Chipotle is piloting frictionless rewards for Apple Pay in-store, which Boatwright expects to roll out across the chain in 2027. Comparable restaurant sales rose 2.1%, according to the earnings report.

**Why it matters:** removing the need to scan a card is a small change that depends on payment and loyalty systems working together reliably.

*Source: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/chipotle-rewards-drives-record-share-loyalty-sales/873236/) · 2 min read · Nov 2*

## Testing

### Why are good developers so wary of agentic development?

Rachel Laycock, CTO at Thoughtworks, argues that experienced developers are not hesitant to use coding assistants, but to let agents do all the execution without proven guardrails. She describes a week where senior engineers had agents build an airline application with no hand-written code. They reached about 80% and spent roughly two thirds of their time at the whiteboard and one third on the harness. The application ran, but the build was frequently red, which she says undermines trust in the checks. She says the last mile needs rethinking, and expects continuous delivery and DevOps to see a resurgence.

**Why it matters:** it points to build signals, tests and pipelines as the part that determines whether agent-written code can be trusted.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/rachels-ramblings/good-devs-wary.html) · 9 min read · Oct 29*

### Fowler's fragments: the gap in how people see LLMs

Martin Fowler collects several observations. He cites Andrej Karpathy on a widening gap in how people perceive LLM capabilities, and reports from Foocamp that large enterprises appear to be using only a small part of the technology, with data readiness and security as reasons. He also quotes Adam Tornhill on agentic coding and Laurie Voss comparing today's AI investment to 1997. These are other people's views, relayed by Fowler.

**Why it matters:** it is a reminder that experience with these tools varies widely, which affects how teams should set expectations.

*Source: [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-10-28.html) · 9 min read · Oct 28*

## Takeaway

Agents are producing far more code and change, and several pieces this week point at what surrounds the code: build signals, rollback paths, review and vendor terms. The common question is whether the checks can keep up with the speed.
